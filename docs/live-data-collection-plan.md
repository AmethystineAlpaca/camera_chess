# Live-data collection plan


**Status:** Planning only. No collector, labeling UI, training change, or runtime change implemented in this entry.

### Findings reported by the user

- Overall experience is good. In the latest game, accepted recognitions were usually correct and Fix recognition was essentially unused. This is qualitative feedback, not measured accuracy or human certification of every board.
- Recognition becomes harder late in a game with fewer pieces. The user often has to lift/tilt the physical board toward a more frontal camera view before recognition proceeds. The main complaint is failure/delay to accept a position, rather than frequent wrong accepted positions.

### Hypotheses — unverified

- Sparse/endgame positions or the deployed oblique view may be underrepresented in training. This could involve the frozen image recognizer, board geometry/orientation, CRF scoring, candidate coverage, acceptance thresholds, or temporal voting; CRF data shortage is only one hypothesis.
- Lifting the board changes geometry, scale, lighting and piece visibility together. It does not by itself identify the cause. Piece self-occlusion from the camera angle must be distinguished from temporary hand occlusion.

### Collection and labeling plan

1. **Capture every actual inference as an immutable observation, before any acceptance filter.** Record session/game ID, camera-stream epoch (sequence numbers can restart), frame ID, capture timestamp and monotonic time, exact source-image hash, input preprocessing/crop/geometry/orientation and their versions, classifier/CRF checkpoint hashes, class order and square order. Keep the original image bytes paired with the exact original `64 × 13` probabilities and raw argmax `64 × 1`; never recreate that pairing from the latest image after inference.
2. **Record each controller evaluation separately.** Attach its own decision ID, observation ID, pre-evaluation context/reference revision, actual reference `64 × 1` plus full FEN, turn, pending move, corrections and physical-piece proxies. Freeze these before controller state changes. Save CRF top proposal `64 × 1` even when rejected, operation/move, candidate count, scores/posterior/gap/visual fit, each gate outcome, votes and final accept/hold/bypass reason. Missing CRF output is null with a reason, never replaced by the eventual accepted board. One observation may have multiple evaluations under different contexts.
3. **Retain a synchronized visual timeline.** Prefer a bounded rolling clip buffer plus exact inference JPEGs. Persist delayed/failed episodes from before the move through stable acceptance, including the lifting event; flush timed-out, abandoned and session-ending failures even when acceptance never occurs. Retain representative ordinary-success and unchanged-board episodes too. A clip carries its frame/time index; video timestamps alone are insufficient to identify the exact classifier image. Queue overflow, missing images and dropped frames are explicit, never silently substituted. Set storage limits and preserve already reviewed examples before evicting unreviewed material. Training sampling and evaluation retention are separate: designate complete pilot/evaluation sessions in advance and retain their continuous input timeline, timestamps and control events; selected clips alone cannot support complete-game replay or an unbiased live failure rate.
4. **Perform quick visibility screening in an asynchronous/offline data worker, outside live recognition decisions.** Estimate board coverage, blur, motion, hand/foreign-object occlusion and geometry changes using image/clip evidence. Output clear/occluded/uncertain and supporting metadata. Low classifier confidence must not mean occluded: those clear difficult frames are the desired examples. Piece self-occlusion is a useful difficulty attribute, not automatic exclusion. Preserve compact event metadata for all evaluated frames; use screening and sampling to choose retained images/clips and training eligibility. Audit both retained and discarded samples because a screening heuristic cannot guarantee visibility.
5. **Segment physical positions before assigning labels.** Use the clip to mark pre-move unchanged board, hand/move transition, post-move settled board, camera/board lifting, and any later move or correction. A confirmed target may propagate backward only inside a visually verified interval with identical physical piece placement, clear visibility and unambiguous square mapping. Require review when the board shifts or orientation changes; recompute geometry per view. A last successful frame must never label the whole preceding waiting interval. Ambiguous boundaries, intermediate capture/castling positions, resets, undo/load, promotions, proxies and multiple missed moves remain unlabeled or separately reviewed.
6. **Separate predictions from truth.** Track application acceptance, human board confirmation, partial corrections and interval verification as separate fields: these can coexist. Application acceptance, legal moves and no use of Fix recognition are supporting evidence, not ground truth. For trusted full-board targets, show a contact sheet/clip with the proposed 64-square board and obtain explicit full-board confirmation; verify the propagation interval as well. Plan this as optional post-game batch review, without mandatory confirmations during play. A verified initial board plus a visually reviewed move sequence can establish later labels, provided each physical transition and any off-script action is checked; the application's own PGN alone is insufficient. Existing correction actions label explicitly edited squares only unless a full-board review is performed. Maintain a square-label mask and confirmation provenance. Unreviewed accepted positions can retain provisional pseudo-labels, but are excluded from the trusted dataset by default.
7. **Version labels and corrections without overwriting evidence.** Each label links to exact observation IDs or an explicitly reviewed interval, target `64 × 1`, label mask, reviewer, time, source and revision. Each correction event links the frozen reviewed image/board to before/after arrays, changed squares and affected state revisions. Record whether a correction occurred, whether it changes an earlier accepted state, and whether related labels were invalidated; support delayed corrections. Logical chess identity and actual visible substitute pieces are separate labels. Unresolved examples cannot enter the trusted training export.
8. **Validate export integrity.** Check uniqueness, image hash, complete finite normalized `64 × 13`, explicit class/square ordering, image orientation/mapping, decision/reference revisions, label masks, interval membership and correction supersession. Both reference truth and target truth need independent provenance. Keep the actual runtime reference even if wrong; optionally attach a separately verified previous state for training the correct-reference CRF. Wrong-reference cases are recovery/evaluation examples rather than silently repaired runtime records. Audit whether the verified target exists in the candidate set; a missing target is a coverage failure, not a scoring-training example.
9. **Sample and evaluate by independent episodes/games.** Retain a small proposed starting budget of 3–8 distinct clear frames per verified position: failed oblique view, just-after-move, pre-acceptance, successful lifted view and normal successes where available. Treat this as a tunable collection budget, not an established optimum. Include unchanged boards, early/middle/endgame and both player/computer turns. Cap contributions per episode so long failures and repeated empty squares do not dominate. Partition entire games/sessions and near-duplicate setups/camera conditions together, never neighboring frames across train/test.

### Volume and expected usefulness

One float32 `64 × 13` array is 3,328 bytes. At an illustrative 2–4 actual inferences/second, this is about 24–48 MB/hour in decimal units before metadata or JSON overhead. A 100–300 KB retained JPEG at every such inference would consume about 0.72–4.32 GB/hour; clips add storage and require a measured bitrate budget. These image sizes/rates are planning assumptions, not measurements of this session. The current capture source throttles JPEG writes to at most roughly 8/second and the inference reader may skip captures, so captured frames, evaluated frames and independent positions are different counts.

For an illustrative 80–120 plies/game and 3–8 selected frames/position, expect roughly 240–960 selected observations/game before exclusions, plus separately budgeted unchanged-position controls. Several thousand frames from ten games are still only hundreds to about a thousand move episodes. The existing 200,000 synthetic observations are not 200,000 independent real camera positions.

Verified in-domain hard examples are plausible useful data, but improvement is not established. More duplicated frames do not necessarily help; label drift, pseudo-label feedback, incorrect references and a failure-only distribution can hurt. CRF fitting cannot recover visual information absent from the input or select a target excluded by candidate generation. Preserve images for future recognizer/geometry work even when only probabilities are used for CRF training.

### Second review — implementation constraints and acceptance criteria

**Code findings:** `lite_live.py` already pairs the original JPEG and probabilities in `observation.bin`, but this is a latest-value file, not an archive. A consumer polling that file can miss inference results. `lite_live_server.py` invokes `audit.observe` after `game.observe`, so the generic state snapshot can already be post-commit; copying it alone would associate the wrong previous state with a transition. Collection must originate at the inference producer, with a separate pre-evaluation controller snapshot and explicit linking. An inference not consumed by the controller has no runtime CRF decision; any later replay result must be marked as offline-derived.

**A specific alternative to the CRF-data hypothesis:** When automatic orientation is enabled, `observe_crf` calls `observe_orientation` before invoking the CRF. Orientation uses attention-derived square geometry and has its own fit/view and temporal gates. A view held there never reaches CRF scoring. Sparse-position geometry instability is still only a hypothesis, but logs must include orientation evidence and the earliest blocking stage. Record raw gate values and thresholds, the squares responsible for occupancy contradictions, temporal supporting frame IDs/reset reasons, and whether the controller actually committed after CRF stability. A CRF-stable proposal and a game move commit are different events.

**Define the previous state precisely:** It is the reference actually supplied to that decision, not the previous video frame or retrospectively inferred previous move. The same physical target can appear with reference A while waiting, then reference B after acceptance. Export these as distinct `(Q, reference, target)` examples; do not assign one previous state to an entire physical-position interval. Never attach a game identity to a producer frame solely from its wall-clock timestamp: capture stream identity and controller-consumption context must both be recorded, including reset/load boundaries.

**Keep difficult data reviewable:** Automated screening proposes retention and labeling intervals; absence of detected motion or hands is not proof of unchanged pieces. Keep a small random audit sample of rejected/uncertain images and image-quality scores, including genuinely clear but misclassified frames. The 3–8-frame budget applies to training export, not to the evidence needed to review an episode. A bounded buffer can lose an unusually long failed episode; persist it incrementally or mark its missing prefix and prohibit labels from crossing the gap. A gap across a lift, occlusion or move prevents automatic label propagation unless independent evidence resolves it.

**Operational scope:** Use a bounded asynchronous collection queue, with disk writing and visibility screening outside the game lock/inference critical path. Measure recorder overhead and dropped-event counts during the pilot. Labels and linked samples become exportable only after their referenced artifacts are durably written and validated; interrupted writes remain incomplete. Save collection/selection policy versions so subsequent sampling bias can be audited. Start the pilot with 3–5 games as a workflow/volume check, not a statistical claim of model improvement.

**Acceptance checks before trusted export:** Explicitly exercise an ordinary move; a long failure followed by lifting; a failure with no eventual success; an unchanged board; a hand/partial capture; late correction/rollback; camera restart; reset/load while inference is in flight; missing image/disk interruption; and a frame held before CRF. Each case must either reconstruct the exact image, runtime reference, decision and reviewed physical target, or remain explicitly incomplete/unverified. Structural checks prevent mechanical mismatches; they cannot guarantee semantic label correctness. Export only reviewed labels and report review coverage and exclusions.
